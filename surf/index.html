🏄 Surf

Boards, waves & lineup etiquette — learn, then quiz yourself.

Key concepts

Start on a foamie

A long, wide foam board ("foamie", 8–9 ft) is stable, forgiving and safe. Shortboards are for later — learning on one is like learning to drive in a race car.

Whitewater first

Begin in the whitewater — the foamy broken part of the wave near shore. It's slower and predictable. Green waves (unbroken faces) come after you can pop up reliably.

The pop-up

From lying down to standing in one explosive motion — hands flat by your chest, push up, swing feet under you. No knees-first: knees on the board is the classic beginner stall.

Priority & dropping in

The surfer closest to the peak (where the wave is breaking) has the right of way. Taking off in front of someone already riding is called dropping in — the cardinal sin of the lineup.

Rip currents

Caught in one? Don't fight it. Swim parallel to shore until you're out of the pull, then ride in. Rip currents don't pull you under — panic and exhaustion do.
